More

Computer science takes up most of my week. This is the rest of it: where I have been, what I watch, and what I eat.

Travel

  • Eight states, so far

    United States

    New York, Massachusetts, Virginia, Utah, Nevada, Washington, Florida and New Hampshire. Utah and Nevada were the ones that surprised me most, having grown up somewhere that looks nothing like that.

  • Southeast Asia

    Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore

    Three countries close together on a map that feel nothing alike once you are there. Singapore in particular is a strange and impressive thing to walk around after growing up in Mumbai.

  • Home

    India

    I have travelled across a good number of states at home. India is less one country than a set of them stacked together, and the food changes faster than the language does.

Watching

  • Interstellar

    Christopher Nolan, 2014

    The one I keep going back to. The time dilation on Miller's planet is the rare case of a film making a physics idea land emotionally rather than just explaining it.

  • The Dark Knight

    Christopher Nolan, 2008

    I am a Batman person first. This is the one that holds up best on a rewatch, mostly because the Joker never gets an origin and the film is better for refusing to give him one.

  • The Marvel films

    Marvel Studios, 2008 onwards

    Being a Batman fan and a Marvel fan at the same time is apparently a controversial position. I have never seen the problem.

  • 3 Idiots

    Rajkumar Hirani, 2009

    Every engineering student in India has seen it. Watching it again after actually finishing an engineering degree is a different experience.

Food

  • Indian

    Home cooking

    Moving countries is a fast way to learn to cook. I started with the things I missed most and worked outwards. Dal is the benchmark: simple enough that there is nowhere to hide, and different every time depending on how patient you are with the tempering.

  • Thai and Burmese

    Worth the detour

    Burmese food is the one I recommend to people who think they have eaten everything in this part of the world. The tea leaf salad alone is worth the trip.

  • Nepali

    Close to home, not the same

    Familiar enough to Indian food that you expect no surprises, and then the momos and the thukpa are their own thing entirely.

  • Italian and American

    Boston

    What I have been eating since I moved. Boston does both well, and the North End is a short walk from most places I need to be.
